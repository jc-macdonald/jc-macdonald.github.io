Reviewer and collaborator profile

Evidence-linked areas for grant review and scientific collaboration.

I am a computational scientist and Assistant Scientist at Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. My overarching interest is how ecological and landscape change influences the movement and connectivity of hosts, pathogens and cultural traits, and through them the persistence and evolution of structured populations. My established work addresses within- and between-host dynamics and cultural transmission, and landscape-scale questions are a developing direction. I build mechanistic models, inference methods and research software for these questions and for decisions under uncertainty. This page is a concise guide to areas in which I can review proposals or contribute to a scientific collaboration; my full CV provides the complete record.

Last substantively reviewed: 24 October 2026.

Review areas

Established areas

  • Viral and immunological modeling, including within-host viral growth, immune response and antibody kinetics, and links to transmission between hosts.
  • Mechanistic modeling of infectious disease dynamics, including within-host, population, spatial, stochastic and multi-scale models, with published applications in infectious disease and ecology.
  • Bayesian and simulation-based inference, including calibration, incomplete data, uncertainty assessment and data assimilation.
  • Model, forecast and decision evaluation, including operational scenario modeling, numerical qualification, validation and comparisons under stated scientific, cost and resource constraints.

Developing and collaborative areas

  • Landscape and metapopulation eco-evolutionary models, asking how landscape change, movement and connectivity shape persistence and divergence across populations.
  • Scientific machine learning for dynamical systems, focused on methods that incorporate or evaluate scientific structure.
  • Decision-focused observation, monitoring and experimental design, developed jointly with domain and field teams that define the scientific decision, feasible measurements and costs.

“Developing” identifies an active methods direction, not a claim of the same depth of evidence as the established areas.

Selected evidence

  • Operational respiratory-virus scenario modeling, with attributable hub repositories and reports where public.
  • Published infectious-disease, cultural and ecological models, including cross-scale foot-and-mouth disease studies, cultural transmission across Austronesian-speaking societies and an NPZD model of harmful algal blooms.
  • Released research software for model specification, deterministic and stochastic simulation, replicated study comparison, and incomplete-data analysis.
  • Computed examples with synthetic inputs, executable sources, numerical checks and pinned provenance.

Scope

My role is mathematical, statistical and computational. Collaborations on monitoring or instrument settings require domain partners to define feasible protocols, calibration procedures and field constraints. I do not present clinical trial conduct, wet-laboratory virology, wildlife field sampling or oceanographic instrument engineering as independent areas of expertise.

For a review request or an initial collaboration discussion, please include the scientific area, decision or review criterion, expected time commitment and any conflict-of-interest constraints. Email me or consult my publications and research software.
