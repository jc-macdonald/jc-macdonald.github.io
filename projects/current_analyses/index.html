Research in progress

Ongoing work on structural scores, scientific machine learning and disease-modeling applications.

My current research connects mechanistic modeling, Bayesian inference and evaluation for decisions under partial observation. The projects below are ongoing. These summaries describe their questions and aims; public papers and software are linked where available.

Structural scores and scientific prediction

I am developing structural scores to assess predictions against declared scientific constraints, including checks that can be made before the outcome is observed. A forecast of a proportion, for example, should respect its stated bounds.

The work asks how to report structural compliance alongside predictive accuracy, and how to make the assumptions and limits of those checks clear. Predictive scoring and structural evaluation address different questions: passing a constraint check alone does not establish predictive skill or scientific validity. Methods and companion software are in development.

Inference and calibration

op_infer connects fitting tools, simulation-based checks and replicated studies for incomplete or noisy observations. The research software remains private, pre-alpha and unreleased while its scientific applications are assessed.

Scientific machine learning and incomplete data

I am exploring how scientific constraints can guide learning from sparse observations of dynamical systems, with attention to the relationship between reconstruction, uncertainty and scientific interpretation. This methodological work is ongoing.

A related thread asks how many latent dimensions an incomplete dataset can support. The released VBPCApy software provides Bayesian dimensionality reduction; pp-eigentest is ongoing research on signal-rank selection, building on a 2024 preprint.

Application studies

Dengue within-host dynamics. Current work develops inference and model assessment for viral and immune responses across successive infections and their relationship to antibody-dependent enhancement. It builds on an earlier preprint.

COVID-19 observation noise. Controlled simulation studies examine how assumptions about surveillance error affect inferred dynamics, predictive uncertainty, and forecast assessment.

Diphtheria outbreak vaccination. Ongoing modeling compares the timing and effects of response strategies under uncertain transmission and immunity.

CCHF surveillance and control. Cross-scale modeling investigates which wildlife and livestock measurements inform disease risk and control choices, alongside their field costs.

Working repositories remain private while the analyses mature. Public artifacts will be added as they are released. See operational modeling for a separate account of my contributions to ongoing forecasting and policy-support programs.
