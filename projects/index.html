Research and software

The questions and study systems I work on, and the models and software I build to study them.

I study how ecological and landscape change influences the movement and connectivity of hosts, pathogens and cultural traits, and through them the persistence and evolution of structured populations. The overarching interest is landscape and metapopulation eco-evolutionary dynamics. My established work addresses within- and between-host dynamics and cultural transmission, and landscape-scale questions are a developing direction. Models, inference methods and software are my tools for these questions, and they apply beyond these systems.

Research questions

  • How do processes within hosts shape transmission and persistence? My buffalo studies of three foot-and-mouth disease virus strains (SAT-1, -2 and -3) indicate that viral replication rate correlates with transmission among buffalo and that the adaptive immune activation rate determines the infectious period. A carrier-transmission model, on which I am a co-author, extends this to persistent infection. As senior personnel on a multi-institution project linking cells to landscapes in African buffalo, I develop and parameterize the within-host models. The SAT serotypes, for which African buffalo are a wildlife host, have recently spread beyond sub-Saharan Africa. SAT-1 was reported for the first time in nine countries from Egypt to Mongolia in 2025 and 2026, including Cyprus, and again in Greece, Lebanon and Türkiye after sixty years (WOAH, August 2026). The buffalo estimates describe infection in buffalo; transmission in cattle and small ruminants was not studied. The same within-host-to-population approach underlies my dengue and Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever applications.
  • How do landscape, movement and contact shape spread, persistence and divergence across populations? This is a developing direction. Published work relates cultural variation to geography and language among Austronesian-speaking societies, without modeling landscape change. Collaborative work in progress models Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever transmission between livestock and wildlife hosts, and spatial structure is a possible extension if movement data allow. The range of this virus’s main vectors is changing. Hyalomma ticks are endemic in, or have been detected at least once in, 40 European countries (Parvage et al. 2025), and Spain reported two locally acquired human cases by July 2026 (ECDC). A bighorn sheep project asks how immune phenotypes vary among metapopulations.
  • How should we measure, vaccinate and decide under uncertainty? For Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever, I ask which wildlife and livestock measurements would change a surveillance or control decision, given their field costs. Modeling in practice describes outbreak-response vaccination and respiratory-virus scenario work, and trade-study supports comparing study designs.

Earlier work includes a COVID-19 model of testing, lockdown speed and fatigue across 55 U.S. jurisdictions, and theory on hysteresis and Hopf bifurcation in a plankton model of harmful algal blooms.

Study systems

  • Foot-and-mouth disease virus in African buffalo. A first-author within-host model and a co-authored carrier-transmission model, developed with collaborators in the Jolles Lab and a multi-institution project.
  • Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever virus in livestock and wildlife. Collaborative transmission models and surveillance design; modeling in progress.
  • Human cultural transmission. Published analysis of networks and clusters of cultural traits, with a methods preprint on incomplete cultural data.
  • Bighorn sheep immune ecology. Collaborative analysis in progress.

Methods and software

The projects below provide software for these questions and describe ongoing methods research. OP System, OP Engine, op_infer and trade-study are general-purpose tools for dynamical systems and study comparison; their scope is not limited to infectious disease or public health. Health, ecological, environmental and social systems provide applications of the same underlying methods.

The pages keep three kinds of status separate. Lifecycle labels say whether software is released or still in development. Evidence labels identify the support: linked publications, operational work, executable software or explicitly identified work in progress. Example type says whether an illustration uses observed, synthetic or constructed inputs. None of these labels, by itself, establishes scientific validity for a new application.

Two further questions concern methods.

  • What can we learn from incomplete data? My cultural transmission research uses statistical summaries of cultural traits. VBPCApy and signal-rank research develop tools for incomplete datasets.
  • How should we assess models and compare studies? Current methods research examines scientific constraints and inference. The replicated design example shows a synthetic comparison of estimation error and assumed cost.

The software packages can be used independently or in combination.

How the OP stack compares

OP System and OP Engine bring to Python the separation of model specification from numerical method that odin2 and dust2 provide in R and that Catalyst and ModelingToolkit provide in Julia. A specification is configuration rather than code, so a scenario pipeline can validate it before a run. It is compiled once, and the array library (NumPy, JAX or PyTorch) is chosen when the model is called, so the same model can be evaluated over a parameter grid or differentiated without being rewritten. Model authors can compare a compiled model with equations they write independently. The same specifications run in operational scenario projections through FlepiMoP2 and in research analyses.

For several needs another tool is the better choice. The table states where the stack fits and where another tool does more.

Need Established tools The OP stack
Reaction networks run as ordinary differential equations, stochastic differential equations or jump processes Catalyst (Julia); GillesPy2 and PySB (Python) Ordinary differential equations, exact stochastic simulation and tau-leaping from one specification; no chemical Langevin equations
Adaptive, differentiable solves in JAX Diffrax Derivatives through fixed-step methods only; the adaptive step controller is not traced
Epidemic models written in a domain-specific language, with particle filters and Markov chain Monte Carlo odin2, dust2 and monty (R) Python and Array API based; the inference layer, op_infer, is in development
Agent-based or renewal-equation models Starsim and LASER; PyRenew and EpiNow2 Not provided; the stack covers population-level compartmental, spatial and reaction-network models

Five further examples (a parameter ensemble by vectorization, a derivative through the solver, deterministic and exact stochastic runs of one specification, spatial convergence to an analytic solution, and vectorized steady-state solves) are on the computed examples page, with their checks and limits. Each layer of the stack is a separate package with its own release and evidence. trade-study compares replicated designs, and op_infer (in development) adds inference adapters and calibration checks. The stage labels in the software guide describe maturity, not validity.
