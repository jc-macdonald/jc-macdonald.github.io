Joshua C. Macdonald

Assistant Scientist · Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health

Baltimore, Maryland

I develop mathematical models, statistical methods and software to understand scientific processes and inform decisions under uncertainty. I study how systems change over time, how well models describe their behavior, and what measurements can tell us about them.

I am an Assistant Scientist at the International Vaccine Access Center, Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. With the Atlantic Coast Center for Infectious Disease Dynamics and Analytics (ACCIDDA), I develop infectious disease models and software for scenario projections and public health planning. I also collaborate with the Jolles Lab on wildlife and livestock disease surveillance.

Email: jmacdo16@jh.edu · ORCID

Previously, I was a Zuckerman STEM Leadership Fellow at Tel Aviv University, working with Yoav Ram on incomplete cultural and genetic data. I hold a PhD in Mathematics from the University of Louisiana at Lafayette and a BA in Archaeology from UNC Greensboro.

Research in progress

My current methods research asks how measurements and scientific assumptions affect what we can learn about a system.

  • Structural scores. Reporting whether predictions satisfy stated scientific constraints, including checks made before the predicted outcome is observed.
  • Inference and calibration. Estimating unknown model quantities and assessing fitting procedures with simulated data. The op_infer research software is private, pre-alpha and unreleased.
  • Incomplete data and latent structure. Investigating how many patterns can be distinguished from noise in incomplete data, building on VBPCApy and a 2024 preprint.

Current application studies address dengue within-host dynamics, observation noise, outbreak vaccination and Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever surveillance. These summaries describe ongoing questions; public papers and artifacts are linked as they become available.

Computations and software

I build reusable tools for model specification, simulation, inference and study comparison. Projects use the pieces they need. OP System and OP Engine support spatial fields, nonlocal interactions, compartmental dynamics and stochastic reactions.

Software and project descriptions cover the packages and their development stages. Computed examples include stochastic ensembles, a replicated design comparison, numerical inputs and checks. Figure downloads provide vector graphics and editable sources.
