I study how models can inform interventions, surveillance, and data collection when the underlying system is only partly observed. I develop simulation, statistical inference, numerical methods, and model-evaluation software for health, environmental, and earth sciences. A recurring question is how assumptions about the system and its measurements affect the conclusions we draw.

Currently I am an Assistant Scientist at the International Vaccine Access Center at Johns Hopkins (postdoctoral scholar there 2024–2026), where I develop operational infectious disease forecasting and decision support systems for ACCIDDA, a CDC Center for Forecasting and Outbreak Analytics collaboration. This work contributes scenario projections to the CDC Flu Scenario Modeling Hub, which supports public health resource allocation and intervention timing. I also collaborate with the Jolles Lab at Oregon State University on cross-scale modeling of Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever (CCHF) in wildlife and livestock systems, designing surveillance strategies that balance information gain against field cost. Previously I was a Zuckerman STEM Leadership Fellow at Tel Aviv University, working with Yoav Ram on Bayesian dimensionality reduction for incomplete cultural and genetic datasets.

I hold a PhD in Mathematics from the University of Louisiana at Lafayette and a BA in Archaeology from UNC Greensboro. Both inform my work on inference from incomplete records.

The common structure

Many of these projects distinguish an underlying process from how it is measured. Disease prevalence, nutrient cycling, and cultural trait frequencies are inferred from observations that may be incomplete or noisy. The figure below shows this distinction across four domains. Each application has its own observation assumptions and validation requirements.

I develop reusable tools for specifying models, simulating observations, fitting parameters, and comparing study designs. Reusing software can reduce implementation work, while scientific assumptions and validation remain specific to each application.

The packages have distinct roles in this workflow.

  • Describing models. OP System turns readable model definitions into calculations. It supports spatial structure, interactions between groups and random events, including PDE, nonlocal and reaction models.
  • Running simulations. OP Engine computes how modeled systems change over time and space. It supports deterministic dynamics, random events, repeated simulations and quantities such as population counts.
  • Learning from data. op_infer estimates unknown model quantities and checks fitting procedures using simulated data. It builds on established statistical libraries and remains private, pre-alpha and unreleased.
  • Coordinating campaigns. FlepiMoP2 organizes simulation runs across places and scenarios, connecting reusable models, simulation tools and data storage.
  • Comparing choices. trade-study compares model settings, experiments and data-collection plans through repeated simulations. It helps assess uncertainty and weigh competing goals such as accuracy, information and cost.
  • Finding patterns. vbpca-py summarizes incomplete datasets with a smaller set of patterns, using a Bayesian approximation to estimate uncertainty. pp-eigentest investigates how many patterns the data can support.

OP System, OP Engine, trade-study and FlepiMoP2 have public alpha releases. VBPCApy is beta. These development labels describe software maturity; numerical checks and scientific assessment remain specific to the method and application.

Research aims

Current methodological work examines scientific constraints on predictions, parameter inference under realistic observation designs, and latent structure in incomplete data. Application studies use these methods to compare interventions and surveillance choices.

Research in progress

  • Structural scores and scientific prediction. Assessing predictions against stated scientific constraints, including checks before the outcome is known.
  • Inference and calibration with op_infer. Research software for parameter inference, calibration checks and replicated studies. Validation for specific applications is ongoing.
  • Incomplete data and latent structure. Research on signal-rank selection for incomplete data, building on the released VBPCApy tools and a 2024 preprint.

Current application studies address dengue within-host dynamics, COVID-19 observation noise, diphtheria vaccination, and CCHF surveillance. These summaries describe ongoing questions and aims; papers and public research artifacts are linked as they become available.

Methods & Expertise

My work uses these methods to study interventions, compare surveillance strategies, and assess the value of additional observations.

Modeling. Differential-equation, stochastic, hybrid and agent-based models; machine learning with scientific constraints; multivariate data analysis and dimensionality reduction; stability, bifurcation, and sensitivity analysis; parameter identifiability and inverse problems.

Inference. Bayesian hierarchical models; variational and simulation-based inference; data assimilation; uncertainty quantification; model calibration and validation; proper scoring rules and forecast evaluation.

Scientific computing. Python, Julia, C++, Stan, R, MATLAB. High-performance numerical solvers; operator splitting; reproducible workflows (Git, CI/CD, containerization); data pipelines and exploratory analysis.
